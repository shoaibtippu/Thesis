\chapter{Literature Review} % (fold)
\label{cha:_literature_review}

Securing patient data within the Internet of Medical Things has attracted research effort from several distinct communities. Cryptographers have proposed attribute-based encryption schemes capable of enforcing fine-grained access policies while maintaining data confidentiality even under server compromise. Security engineers have developed authentication protocols with strong identity guarantees for resource-constrained medical devices. Machine learning researchers, meanwhile, have shown that data-driven, adaptive security decisions are feasible in IoMT deployments. Despite this breadth of activity, to the best of the authors' knowledge no existing work links the assessed risk of an access attempt to the cryptographic layer that governs what a session may decrypt. This chapter surveys the most pertinent recent contributions in each area and uses them to identify the specific gap that this thesis addresses. Papers were selected on the basis of direct relevance to at least one of three dimensions: cryptographic access control for IoMT, authentication security in IoMT, or machine learning for security in IoMT, with an emphasis on peer-reviewed journal publications from 2024 onwards.

\subsection{Access Control and Encryption in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_211}

Among the most recent and comprehensive contributions to cryptographic access control for IoMT is the scheme proposed by Ding et al., which presents a blockchain-enhanced anonymous data sharing framework for 6G-enabled smart healthcare incorporating distributed key generation and policy hiding~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Key generation is distributed across blockchain smart contracts using Newton interpolation, removing the dependency on a central trusted authority. The scheme integrates CP-ABE policy hiding with anonymous authentication, ensuring that neither the structure of the access policy nor the requesting user's identity is revealed during data access. It is specifically designed to accommodate the overlapping and evolving role assignments typical of healthcare environments and reports favourable computational efficiency relative to comparable data-sharing schemes.

Liang et al.\ propose a complementary access control scheme for smart healthcare that couples CP-ABE with privacy-preserving authentication and a flexible key revocation mechanism, enabling access rights to be efficiently withdrawn without requiring re-encryption of stored data~\cite{liang2024access}. This work addresses the revocation problem that has historically limited practical deployment of CP-ABE in healthcare settings; however, it makes no use of the context in which credentials are presented. A session holding valid, unrevoked attributes is granted decryption access regardless of how anomalous the login behaviour may appear.

Bharath Babu and Jothi propose a privacy-preserving analytics framework for multi-tenant cloud-hosted healthcare records, combining zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) with a blockchain-based audit layer~\cite{bharathbabu2025zkp}. While the primary focus of this work is analytics rather than fine-grained access control, it is relevant to this subsection because the ZKP component determines what computations may be performed on encrypted records, which is a form of cryptographic enforcement conceptually related to CP-ABE policy gating. The ZKP mechanism enables a party to demonstrate the correctness of a computation over encrypted health data without exposing the underlying records, while the blockchain provides a tamper-evident log of all query activity. Nevertheless, as with the schemes of Ding et al.\ and Liang et al., the authorisation policy remains static: any credential satisfying the policy conditions is granted access irrespective of the context in which it is presented.

Of particular significance to this thesis is a limitation that Ding et al.\ themselves acknowledge: the access decision is static~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Once a user holds a key satisfying the ciphertext policy, decryption is granted unconditionally, with no consideration of the access context. A login attempt from an unrecognised device in a foreign country is treated no differently from a routine access from a known hospital workstation. The authors identify efficient attribute revocation and computational optimisation as open directions for future work, but do not consider the risk profile of the access attempt as an input to the access decision. This static characteristic, which is shared by virtually all CP-ABE schemes in the literature, defines the central limitation that the present thesis sets out to address.

\subsection{Authentication Security in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_212}

Recent cryptographic research has targeted the authentication layer of IoMT systems. Xu et al.\ proposed the anonymity-enhanced sequential multi-signer ring signature (ASMR) scheme, in which patients and clinicians co-sign electronic health records within separate anonymous signature rings~\cite{xu2025asmr}. The scheme achieves both anonymity and traceability in the absence of a central trusted entity, with security proofs established in the random oracle model and significant reductions in computational and communication overhead relative to prior ring signature constructions. It guarantees that only authorised parties have contributed to a given record and that individual signatories cannot be identified from the aggregate signature.

In a related contribution, Xu et al.\ proposed PQ-CLSCL, a lattice-based certificateless signcryption scheme incorporating a linkability mechanism for IoMT~\cite{xu2025pqclscl}. By grounding security in lattice hardness assumptions, PQ-CLSCL achieves resistance to quantum adversaries, which is practically important given the extended retention periods of electronic health records. The linkability feature enables the system to detect and reject replayed messages, providing an additional layer of protection against replay attacks.

Both schemes provide strong identity and data origin guarantees, yet they share a defining limitation: each reaches a binary accept/reject verdict based solely on key material. Valid credentials are accepted; invalid ones are rejected. Neither scheme incorporates the behavioural or environmental context of the access request into its decision. ASMR establishes the identities of those who co-signed a record but provides no information about who seeks to access it later or under what circumstances. The linkability in PQ-CLSCL is sensitive to message repetition, not to the risk profile of the requesting session. A stolen credential bearing a valid signature clears every check in both schemes without raising any alarm. Even risk-adaptive approaches such as that of Mavhemwa et al., which select an authenticator based on assessed contextual risk, stop at the authentication boundary~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. What a session may decrypt once admitted depends only on its attribute set, regardless of the risk level that triggered the authentication decision.

\subsection{Machine Learning for Security in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_213}

Machine learning has been applied with considerable success to security decision-making in IoMT settings. Ullah et al.\ developed an interpretable threat detection framework that combines a GAN (used to synthesise samples for underrepresented attack classes and address class imbalance) with a BiLSTM for modelling temporal attack patterns~\cite{ullah2025interpretable}. SHAP and LIME explanations are incorporated to render individual detection decisions intelligible to clinical users. Evaluated on established IoMT benchmark datasets, the system achieves strong detection performance while satisfying the transparency requirements of clinical environments. This work establishes that ML models can deliver both accurate and explainable security decisions on the heavily imbalanced data characteristic of IoMT, which is a challenge directly relevant to the approach developed in this thesis.

Anjum et al.\ proposed a system that couples a Random Forest classifier with a blockchain-based access control layer for healthcare IoT~\cite{anjum2025blockchain}. The classifier continuously analyses access patterns and flags behaviour indicative of misuse; upon detecting an anomaly, the system triggers a real-time update to the blockchain-enforced access policy. The system demonstrates accurate anomaly detection and low operational latency, showing that ML-driven dynamic access control is feasible in healthcare IoT at scale.

Nowrozy et al.\ propose a tripartite access control model for electronic health records that integrates GPT-based natural language processing, an ontology layer, and context-aware attribute-based access control (CAABAC)~\cite{nowrozy2025caabac}. Although primarily an access control policy engine, the work is included in this subsection because its use of GPT for policy inference and ontology-driven context extraction represents a learned, data-driven approach to access decisions rather than a static rule-based one. The model conditions access on both a user's role attributes and the compliance and situational context present at the time of the request. A critical distinction from the approach proposed in this thesis, however, is that CAABAC operates at the policy-decision layer: it determines whether access should be permitted but does not embed the risk assessment into the cryptographic key material itself. The present thesis enforces context-derived risk attributes within CP-ABE session keys, ensuring that the restriction remains effective even if the overlying policy layer is bypassed or misconfigured.

Mavhemwa et al.\ developed a Na\"{i}ve Bayes-based adaptive continuous authentication prototype targeting young IoMT users on Android devices, drawing on physiological biometrics from smartwatches in conjunction with behavioural, environmental, and contextual signals~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. At each authentication event, the model assesses risk and trust by evaluating device characteristics, network conditions, location data, and user habit patterns alongside demographic factors such as age and medical condition, dynamically selecting the most appropriate authenticator. This work is of direct relevance to the present thesis in that it demonstrates the practical feasibility of risk-based adaptive authentication in an IoMT deployment. The reported false rejection rate of 19\% provides a usability reference point against which the false challenge rate of the risk model developed in this thesis may be contextualised. The principal difference lies in scope: Mavhemwa et al.\ confine their risk assessment to the authentication decision itself, whereas this thesis propagates the risk score into the CP-ABE encryption layer, enforcing the restriction cryptographically within the stored data.

The work most closely aligned with the approach proposed in this thesis is the SmartIoT Hybrid ML model of Saleem et al., which couples a Random Forest classifier with attribute-based authentication for IoT access control~\cite{saleem2025ml}. The model evaluates each access request on the basis of user attributes, login patterns, and behavioural signals, achieving 86\% authentication accuracy and 96\% recall at a response latency of 112\,ms on low-power devices. This demonstrates that the combination of machine learning with attribute-based security mechanisms is both technically feasible and practically effective in IoT environments. However, the scheme operates exclusively at the authentication decision layer: it determines whether to admit or reject a session but exercises no control over what an admitted session may subsequently decrypt. Sensitive data remains accessible to any session that satisfies the classifier, and the access decision carries no cryptographic enforcement at the data storage level.

In summary, all ML-based systems reviewed in this subsection operate at the detection, policy-decision, or authentication layer. Ullah et al.\ generate a threat alert; Anjum et al.\ update an access policy in response to detected anomalies; Nowrozy et al.\ adjust an access decision based on contextual compliance; Mavhemwa et al.\ select a risk-appropriate authenticator; and Saleem et al.\ admit or deny sessions based on attribute-enriched ML scores. None of these systems penetrates the encryption layer. Data remains accessible to any session that clears the detection or decision checkpoint, and policy revisions do not retroactively constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. The case for cryptographic enforcement rests on this observation: a CP-ABE key that is incapable of decrypting sensitive data provides a guarantee that holds independently of whether the detection system has been deceived.

\section{Critical Analysis and Research Gaps}
\label{sec:section_CriticalAnalysis}

The literature reviewed above reveals three specific gaps, each identified in direct correspondence with the problem statement and research questions of this thesis.

\textbf{Static cryptographic access control.} A recent high-performing CP-ABE scheme for IoMT, the scheme of Ding et al.~\cite{ding2026badsabe}, grants decryption rights to any session holding the requisite attributes, irrespective of the context in which access is requested. The encryption layer has no awareness of login behaviour or access risk. A credential stolen from a healthcare worker therefore confers the same cryptographic access as that afforded to the worker themselves, with no mechanism at the data layer to prevent unauthorised decryption.

\textbf{Authentication that stops at the login boundary.} Both ASMR~\cite{xu2025asmr} and PQ-CLSCL~\cite{xu2025pqclscl} give strong guarantees about identity and data integrity, but their protective scope ends at the moment of authentication. Once a session is admitted, neither scheme has any further influence over what it may decrypt. A stolen credential with a valid signature passes every check just as the legitimate owner's credential would.

\textbf{ML-driven security decisions that do not reach the encryption layer.} Ullah et al.~\cite{ullah2025interpretable} and Anjum et al.~\cite{anjum2025blockchain} both demonstrate that machine learning can produce accurate, adaptive security decisions within IoMT environments. However, in each case the system operates at the detection or policy-decision layer, leaving the encryption layer unaffected. Data remains accessible to any session that is admitted or that circumvents the detection point, and policy revisions do not constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. The reviewed literature thus confirms that the gap identified here, namely context-aware cryptographic access control governed by a learned risk model, remains unaddressed.

The present thesis addresses this gap by augmenting the CP-ABE access control framework of Ding et al.~\cite{ding2026badsabe} with a machine learning-derived risk attribute. A risk model is trained on a large-scale real-world login dataset, and its output is mapped to one of three risk levels (low, medium, or high) that are embedded into the user's CP-ABE session key. Sensitive synthetic patient records are encrypted under sensitivity-tiered policies, ensuring that a session assessed as high-risk is unable to decrypt sensitive records and must undergo re-authentication before access is granted.

\section{Summary of the Chapter}
\label{sec:section_LitSum}

This chapter has reviewed the most pertinent recent literature on cryptographic access control for IoMT, using the scheme of Ding et al.\ as the primary reference for assessing what CP-ABE can and cannot achieve in practice. Authentication schemes for IoMT were examined and found, without exception, to treat authentication as a one-time binary decision with no subsequent influence over the decryption layer. Machine learning approaches to IoMT security were surveyed and shown to produce accurate and adaptive decisions, yet none carries those decisions into the encryption layer where data is ultimately protected. Three specific research gaps were identified across these threads, each directly connected to the problem statement, research questions, and objectives of this thesis. The methodology developed to address these gaps is presented in the following chapter.

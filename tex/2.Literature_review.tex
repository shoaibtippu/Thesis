\chapter{Literature Review} % (fold)
\label{cha:_literature_review}

Securing patient data within the Internet of Medical Things has attracted research effort from several distinct communities. Cryptographers have proposed attribute-based encryption schemes capable of enforcing fine-grained access policies while maintaining data confidentiality even under server compromise. Security engineers have developed authentication protocols with strong identity guarantees for resource-constrained medical devices. Meanwhile, machine learning work has shown that IoMT security decisions can adapt to the data they see. No existing work, to the best of the author's knowledge, links the assessed risk of an access attempt to the cryptographic layer that governs what a session may decrypt. This chapter surveys the most pertinent recent contributions in each area and uses them to identify the specific gap that the present thesis addresses. Papers were selected on the basis of direct relevance to at least one of four dimensions: cryptographic access control for IoMT, authentication security in IoMT, risk-based authentication, or machine learning for security in IoMT, with an emphasis on peer-reviewed journal publications from 2024 onwards. Foundational and closely related earlier works are cited where they define the techniques used or are the nearest prior approaches.

\subsection{Access Control and Encryption in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_211}

Ding et al.\ present a blockchain-enhanced framework for anonymous data sharing in 6G-enabled smart healthcare, with distributed key generation and policy hiding~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Blockchain smart contracts share the key generation through Newton interpolation, so the scheme does not rely on a central trusted authority. CP-ABE policy hiding is paired with anonymous authentication, which means a user accessing data reveals neither the structure of the access policy nor their own identity. It is specifically designed to accommodate the overlapping and evolving role assignments typical of healthcare environments and reports lower computational cost than comparable data-sharing schemes.

Liang et al.\ propose a complementary access control scheme for smart healthcare that couples CP-ABE with privacy-preserving authentication and a flexible key revocation mechanism, enabling access rights to be efficiently withdrawn without requiring re-encryption of stored data~\cite{liang2024access}. This work addresses the revocation problem that has historically limited practical deployment of CP-ABE in healthcare settings; however, it makes no use of the context in which credentials are presented. A session holding valid, unrevoked attributes is granted decryption access regardless of how anomalous the login behaviour may appear.

Bharath Babu and Jothi introduce an analytical framework designed to protect sensitive information and preserve user privacy framework for multi-tenant cloud-hosted healthcare records, combining zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) with a blockchain-based audit layer~\cite{bharathbabu2025zkp}. The ZKP component governs what computations may be performed on encrypted records, making it conceptually related to CP-ABE policy gating, though the system's primary focus is analytics. The ZKP mechanism enables a party to demonstrate the correctness of a computation over encrypted health data without exposing the underlying records, while the blockchain provides a tamper-evident log of all query activity. As with the schemes of Ding et al.\ and Liang et al., however, the authorisation policy remains static: any credential satisfying the policy conditions is granted access irrespective of the context in which it is presented.

A limitation evident in the design of Ding et al.\ is that the access decision is static~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Once a user holds a key satisfying the ciphertext policy, decryption is granted unconditionally, with no consideration of the access context. A login attempt from an unrecognised device in a foreign country is treated no differently from a routine access from a known hospital workstation. The design does not consider the risk profile of the access attempt as an input to the access decision. Nearly every CP-ABE scheme in the literature is static in this way, and this is the limitation the thesis addresses.

\subsection{Authentication in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_212}

Other recent cryptographic work focuses on authentication in IoMT systems. Xu et al.\ propose ASMR, a sequential ring signature scheme with enhanced signer anonymity, in which patients and clinicians co-sign electronic health records from within separate anonymous signature rings~\cite{xu2025asmr}. The scheme provides anonymity and traceability together without a central trusted entity, and its security is proved in the random oracle model. The authors also report lower computational and communication overhead than earlier ring signature constructions. It ensures that only authorised parties contribute to a record, while the aggregate signature does not reveal which individual parties signed it.

Xu et al. also propose PQ-CLSCL, a post-quantum certificateless signcryption scheme with linkability for IoMT~\cite{xu2025pqclscl}. Its security rests on the hardness of the Module Learning With Errors (MLWE) lattice problem, so it resists quantum adversaries. That matters in healthcare, where electronic health records are often kept for decades. Because the design is certificateless, no trusted authority holds a copy of a user's private key, which avoids the key-escrow weakness of identity-based schemes. It also removes the certificate management overhead of a conventional public key infrastructure. Signcryption handles encryption and authentication in one operation, which reduces the computational load on resource-constrained IoMT devices. To stop replay attacks, the scheme binds each message to a unique session token and rejects any transmission it has already seen. This check works only on repeated messages. It does not consider the behavioural or environmental context of the session, so an attacker with a stolen credential who sends a new, unrepeated message passes every verification step undetected.

Both schemes give reliable assurance of identity and data origin, but each makes a simple accept or reject decision based only on key material. PQ-CLSCL's linkability check responds only to repeated messages, not to the risk profile of the session. In both schemes, therefore, a stolen credential that produces a valid signature passes every check undetected.

Risk-adaptive approaches such as that of Mavhemwa et al., which select an authenticator based on the assessed contextual risk of a login attempt, reach no further than the authentication boundary~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. What a session may decrypt once admitted depends solely on its attribute set, regardless of the risk level that triggered the authentication challenge. The restriction the authentication system imposes is therefore advisory: it can delay or complicate access, but it cannot prevent a session that clears the authentication step from reaching data it is cryptographically permitted to decrypt.

\subsection{Risk-Based Authentication}
\label{sec:subsection_213rba}

Risk-based authentication (RBA) strengthens password-based login by estimating, for each login attempt, how likely it is to be fraudulent. Freeman et al.\ propose a statistical model that scores a login by comparing its features, such as IP address, autonomous system number and user agent, with the user's own login history and with the global population, so that feature values common overall but new for the user raise the risk~\cite{freeman2016you}. Wiefling et al.\ provide the first long-term evaluation of risk-based authentication (RBA) within a large-scale, real-world online platform, studying 31.3 million login attempts from 3.3 million users over more than one year~\cite{wiefling2023pump}. They classify risk scores into low, medium and high levels, request re-authentication for risky logins, propose a machine learning based method for optimising RBA configuration, and release a synthesised dataset of over 33 million login attempts that preserves the characteristics of the original data to support reproducible RBA research~\cite{wiefling2023pump}. This dataset and the Freeman et al.\ model provide, respectively, the training data and the baseline for the risk model developed in the present thesis.

RBA, however, stops at the login decision. A login judged risky is challenged with an additional factor, and a login that passes is admitted with the full rights of the account. Because the risk score has no effect on what an admitted session can decrypt, RBA cannot limit data exposure once a challenge has been passed or bypassed. This thesis takes the RBA risk level past the login stage and places it in the CP-ABE key, where it controls decryption directly.

\subsection{Machine Learning for Security in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_213}

Nowrozy et al.~\cite{nowrozy2025caabac} and Mavhemwa et al.~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes} are reviewed here but left out of the comparison in Table~\ref{tab:demo_table}, because neither is a CP-ABE scheme. Nowrozy et al.\ build a context-aware policy decision engine, not a cryptographic access control scheme. Mavhemwa et al.\ develop an adaptive authentication prototype that works at login and has no effect on the encryption layer. Both informed the design of this thesis.

Machine learning is already used in practice for IoMT security. Ullah et al.\ develop an interpretable threat detection framework that combines a GAN (used to synthesise samples for underrepresented attack classes and address class imbalance) with a BiLSTM for modelling temporal attack patterns~\cite{ullah2025interpretable}. SHAP and LIME explanations are incorporated to render individual detection decisions intelligible to clinical users. Evaluated on established IoMT benchmark datasets, the system achieves strong detection performance while satisfying the transparency requirements of clinical environments. The system confirms that ML models can produce accurate, explainable decisions on heavily imbalanced IoMT data.

Anjum et al.\ propose a system that couples a Random Forest classifier with a blockchain-based access control layer for healthcare IoT~\cite{anjum2025blockchain}. The classifier continuously analyses access patterns and flags behaviour indicative of misuse; upon detecting an anomaly, the system triggers a real-time update to the blockchain-enforced access policy. The system demonstrates accurate anomaly detection and low operational latency, showing that ML-driven dynamic access control is feasible in healthcare IoT at scale.

Nowrozy et al.\ propose a tripartite access control model for electronic health records that integrates GPT-based natural language processing, an ontology layer, and context-aware attribute-based access control (CAABAC)~\cite{nowrozy2025caabac}. Its use of GPT for policy inference and ontology-driven context extraction makes it a learned, data-driven approach to access decisions rather than a rule-based one. The model conditions access on a user's role attributes and the compliance and situational context present at the time of the request. A critical distinction from the approach proposed in the present thesis, however, is that CAABAC operates at the policy-decision layer: it determines whether access should be permitted but does not embed the risk assessment into the cryptographic key material itself. The present thesis enforces context-derived risk attributes within CP-ABE session keys, ensuring that the restriction remains effective even if the overlying policy layer is bypassed or misconfigured.

Mavhemwa et al.\ develop a Na\"{i}ve Bayes-based adaptive continuous authentication prototype targeting young IoMT users on Android devices, drawing on physiological biometrics from smartwatches in conjunction with behavioural, environmental, and contextual signals~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. At each authentication event, the model weighs risk and trust using device characteristics, network conditions, location, user habits, and demographic factors such as age and medical condition. It then picks the authenticator best suited to that level of risk. The system shows that risk-based adaptive authentication is practical in an IoMT deployment. The reported false rejection rate of 19\% provides a usability reference point against which the false challenge rate of the risk model developed in the present thesis may be contextualised. The principal difference lies in scope: Mavhemwa et al.\ confine their risk assessment to the authentication decision itself, whereas the present thesis propagates the risk score into the CP-ABE encryption layer, enforcing the restriction cryptographically within the stored data.

Saleem et al.\ present the proposed SmartIoT hybrid machine learning framework, which is the most closely related work to the approach proposed in the present thesis~\cite{saleem2025ml}. It couples a Random Forest classifier with attribute-driven authentication mechanisms for regulating access within IoT environments, evaluating each access request on the basis of user-specific characteristics, authentication patterns, and behavioural indicators, and achieves 86\% authentication accuracy and 96\% recall at a response latency of 112\,ms on low-power devices. The result confirms that machine learning and attribute-based security can be combined at a latency suitable for IoT devices with limited computational and processing capabilities. However, the scheme operates exclusively at the authentication decision layer: it determines whether to admit or reject a session but exercises no control over what an admitted session may subsequently decrypt. Sensitive data remains accessible to any session that satisfies the classifier, and the access decision carries no cryptographic enforcement at the data storage level.

All ML-based systems reviewed in this subsection operate at the detection, policy-decision, or authentication layer. Ullah et al.\ generate a threat alert; Anjum et al.\ update an access policy in response to detected anomalies; Nowrozy et al.\ adjust an access decision based on contextual compliance; Mavhemwa et al.\ select a risk-appropriate authenticator; and Saleem et al.\ admit or deny sessions based on attribute-enriched ML scores. None of these systems penetrates the encryption layer. Data remains accessible to any session that clears the detection or decision checkpoint, and policy revisions do not retroactively constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. The case for cryptographic enforcement rests on this observation: a CP-ABE key that is incapable of decrypting sensitive data provides a guarantee that holds independently of whether the detection system has been deceived.

\section{Critical Analysis and Research Gaps}
\label{sec:section_CriticalAnalysis}

\textbf{Static cryptographic access control.} The literature reviewed above reveals three specific gaps, each corresponding directly to the problem statement and research questions of the present thesis. The first gap concerns static cryptographic access control. Ding et al.~\cite{ding2026badsabe} propose one of the strongest recent CP-ABE schemes for IoMT, yet it gives decryption rights to any session holding the required attributes, whatever the context of the request. The encryption layer knows nothing about login behaviour or access risk. A credential stolen from a healthcare worker therefore opens exactly what the worker's own key opens, and nothing at the data layer stops the attacker from decrypting.

\textbf{Authentication that stops at the login boundary.} ASMR~\cite{xu2025asmr} and PQ-CLSCL~\cite{xu2025pqclscl} both give reliable assurance of identity and data integrity, but only up to the point of authentication. After a session is admitted, neither scheme has any say over what it can decrypt. A stolen credential with a valid signature therefore passes every check, exactly as the legitimate owner's would.

\textbf{ML-driven security decisions that do not reach the encryption layer.} Ullah et al.~\cite{ullah2025interpretable} and Anjum et al.~\cite{anjum2025blockchain} show that machine learning can make accurate security decisions in IoMT that adapt to each request. Both systems, however, act at the detection or policy decision layer, and the encryption layer is left unchanged. Data remains accessible to any session that is admitted or that circumvents the detection point, and policy revisions do not constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. Context-aware cryptographic access control governed by a learned risk model remains unaddressed in the literature surveyed here.


The methodology developed in the present thesis to address this gap, covering the risk model, the CP-ABE integration design, and the experimental evaluation protocol, is detailed in Chapter~3.

\section{Summary of the Chapter}
\label{sec:section_LitSum}

This chapter has reviewed the most recent literature on cryptographic access control for IoMT, using the scheme of Ding et al.\ as the primary reference for assessing what CP-ABE can and cannot achieve in practice. Authentication schemes for IoMT were examined and found, without exception, to treat authentication as a one-time binary decision with no subsequent influence over the decryption layer. Machine learning approaches to IoMT security were surveyed and shown to produce accurate and adaptive decisions, yet none carries those decisions into the encryption layer where data is protected. Three specific research gaps were identified across these threads, each directly connected to the problem statement, research questions, and objectives of the present thesis. The methodology developed to address these gaps is presented in the following chapter.

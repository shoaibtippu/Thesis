\chapter{Literature Review} % (fold)
\label{cha:_literature_review}

Securing patient data within the Internet of Medical Things has attracted research effort from several distinct communities. Cryptographers have proposed attribute-based encryption schemes capable of enforcing fine-grained access policies while maintaining data confidentiality even under server compromise. Security engineers have developed authentication protocols with strong identity guarantees for resource-constrained medical devices. Machine learning researchers, meanwhile, have shown that data-driven, adaptive security decisions are feasible in IoMT deployments. No existing work, to the best of the author's knowledge, links the assessed risk of an access attempt to the cryptographic layer that governs what a session may decrypt. This chapter surveys the most pertinent recent contributions in each area and uses them to identify the specific gap that the present thesis addresses. Papers were selected on the basis of direct relevance to at least one of four dimensions: cryptographic access control for IoMT, authentication security in IoMT, risk-based authentication, or machine learning for security in IoMT, with an emphasis on peer-reviewed journal publications from 2024 onwards. Foundational and closely related earlier works are cited where they define the techniques used or are the nearest prior approaches.

The table shows a consistent pattern: works that provide strong cryptographic guarantees (rows 1--5) treat access control as a static, attribute-only decision, while works that apply machine learning (rows 6--8) operate exclusively at the detection or authentication layer and exercise no control over the encryption layer. \subsection{Access Control and Encryption in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_211}

Ding et al.\ present a blockchain-enhanced anonymous data sharing framework for 6G-enabled smart healthcare, incorporating distributed key generation and policy hiding~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Key generation is distributed across blockchain smart contracts using Newton interpolation, removing the dependency on a central trusted authority. The scheme integrates CP-ABE policy hiding with anonymous authentication, ensuring that neither the structure of the access policy nor the requesting user's identity is revealed during data access. It is specifically designed to accommodate the overlapping and evolving role assignments typical of healthcare environments and reports lower computational cost than comparable data-sharing schemes.

Liang et al.\ propose a complementary access control scheme for smart healthcare that couples CP-ABE with privacy-preserving authentication and a flexible key revocation mechanism, enabling access rights to be efficiently withdrawn without requiring re-encryption of stored data~\cite{liang2024access}. This work addresses the revocation problem that has historically limited practical deployment of CP-ABE in healthcare settings; however, it makes no use of the context in which credentials are presented. A session holding valid, unrevoked attributes is granted decryption access regardless of how anomalous the login behaviour may appear.

Bharath Babu and Jothi propose a privacy-preserving analytics framework for multi-tenant cloud-hosted healthcare records, combining zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) with a blockchain-based audit layer~\cite{bharathbabu2025zkp}. The ZKP component governs what computations may be performed on encrypted records, making it conceptually related to CP-ABE policy gating, though the system's primary focus is analytics. The ZKP mechanism enables a party to demonstrate the correctness of a computation over encrypted health data without exposing the underlying records, while the blockchain provides a tamper-evident log of all query activity. As with the schemes of Ding et al.\ and Liang et al., however, the authorisation policy remains static: any credential satisfying the policy conditions is granted access irrespective of the context in which it is presented.

A limitation evident in the design of Ding et al.\ is that the access decision is static~\cite{ding2026badsabe}. Once a user holds a key satisfying the ciphertext policy, decryption is granted unconditionally, with no consideration of the access context. A login attempt from an unrecognised device in a foreign country is treated no differently from a routine access from a known hospital workstation. \textbf{[VERIFY: confirm in the conclusion of Ding et al.\ that they list attribute revocation and optimisation as future work.]} The design does not consider the risk profile of the access attempt as an input to the access decision. This static characteristic, which is shared by virtually all CP-ABE schemes in the literature, defines the central limitation that the present thesis sets out to address.


\subsection{Authentication in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_212}

Recent cryptographic research has targeted the authentication layer of IoMT systems. Xu et al.\ propose the anonymity-enhanced sequential multi-signer ring signature (ASMR) scheme, in which patients and clinicians co-sign electronic health records within separate anonymous signature rings~\cite{xu2025asmr}. The scheme achieves both anonymity and traceability in the absence of a central trusted entity, with security proofs established in the random oracle model and significant reductions in computational and communication overhead relative to prior ring signature constructions. It guarantees that only authorised parties have contributed to a given record and that individual signatories cannot be identified from the aggregate signature.

In a related contribution, Xu et al.\ propose PQ-CLSCL, a post-quantum certificateless signcryption scheme with linkability for IoMT~\cite{xu2025pqclscl}. PQ-CLSCL grounds its security in the hardness of the Module Learning With Errors (MLWE) lattice problem, rendering it resistant to quantum adversaries, which is of practical importance given the decade-scale retention periods typical of electronic health records. The certificateless design avoids the key-escrow vulnerability inherent in identity-based schemes, as no trusted authority holds a copy of the user's private key, while simultaneously eliminating the certificate management overhead of conventional public-key infrastructure. Signcryption combines encryption and authentication in a single efficient operation, reducing the computational footprint on resource-constrained IoMT devices. The linkability mechanism detects and rejects replayed transmissions by binding each message to a unique session token; however, linkability is sensitive to message-level repetition, not to the behavioural or environmental context of the requesting session. A stolen credential presenting a fresh, unrepeated transmission clears every verification step without raising any alarm.

Both schemes provide strong identity and data origin guarantees, yet each reaches a binary accept/reject verdict based solely on key material. Neither incorporates the behavioural or environmental context of the access request into its decision. ASMR establishes the identities of those who co-signed a record but provides no information about who seeks to access it later or under what circumstances. The linkability in PQ-CLSCL is sensitive to message repetition, not to the risk profile of the requesting session. A stolen credential bearing a valid signature clears every check in both schemes without raising any alarm.

Risk-adaptive approaches such as that of Mavhemwa et al., which select an authenticator based on the assessed contextual risk of a login attempt, reach no further than the authentication boundary~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. What a session may decrypt once admitted depends solely on its attribute set, regardless of the risk level that triggered the authentication challenge. The restriction the authentication system imposes is therefore advisory: it can delay or complicate access, but it cannot prevent a session that clears the authentication step from reaching data it is cryptographically permitted to decrypt.

\subsection{Risk-Based Authentication}
\label{sec:subsection_213rba}

Risk-based authentication (RBA) strengthens password-based login by estimating, for each login attempt, how likely it is to be fraudulent. Freeman et al.\ propose a statistical model that scores a login by comparing its features, such as IP address, autonomous system number and user agent, with the user's own login history and with the global population, so that feature values common overall but new for the user raise the risk~\cite{freeman2016you}. Wiefling et al.\ provide the first long-term analysis of RBA on a real-world large-scale online service, studying 31.3 million login attempts from 3.3 million users over more than one year~\cite{wiefling2023pump}. They classify risk scores into low, medium and high levels, request re-authentication for risky logins, propose a machine learning based method for optimising RBA configuration, and release a synthesised dataset of over 33 million login attempts that preserves the characteristics of the original data to support reproducible RBA research~\cite{wiefling2023pump}. This dataset and the Freeman et al.\ model provide, respectively, the training data and the baseline for the risk model developed in the present thesis.

RBA, however, ends at the login decision. A login judged risky is challenged with an additional factor, and a login that passes is admitted with the full rights of the account. The risk score does not influence what an admitted session may decrypt, so RBA cannot limit data exposure once a challenge is passed or bypassed. The present thesis carries the RBA risk level beyond the login boundary into the CP-ABE key, so that it governs decryption directly.

\subsection{Machine Learning for Security in IoMT}
\label{sec:subsection_213}

Two additional works reviewed in this subsection, Nowrozy et al.~\cite{nowrozy2025caabac} and Mavhemwa et al.~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}, are examined for completeness but are not included in the primary comparison of Table~\ref{tab:demo_table}. Nowrozy et al.\ contribute a context-aware policy-decision engine rather than a cryptographic access control scheme, while Mavhemwa et al.\ develop an adaptive authentication prototype that operates at the login boundary without influencing the encryption layer. Both works inform the present thesis but fall outside the core CP-ABE comparison criteria used to construct the table.

Machine learning has seen real application in IoMT security. Ullah et al.\ develop an interpretable threat detection framework that combines a GAN (used to synthesise samples for underrepresented attack classes and address class imbalance) with a BiLSTM for modelling temporal attack patterns~\cite{ullah2025interpretable}. SHAP and LIME explanations are incorporated to render individual detection decisions intelligible to clinical users. Evaluated on established IoMT benchmark datasets, the system achieves strong detection performance while satisfying the transparency requirements of clinical environments. The system confirms that ML models can produce accurate, explainable decisions on heavily imbalanced IoMT data.

Anjum et al.\ propose a system that couples a Random Forest classifier with a blockchain-based access control layer for healthcare IoT~\cite{anjum2025blockchain}. The classifier continuously analyses access patterns and flags behaviour indicative of misuse; upon detecting an anomaly, the system triggers a real-time update to the blockchain-enforced access policy. The system demonstrates accurate anomaly detection and low operational latency, showing that ML-driven dynamic access control is feasible in healthcare IoT at scale.

Nowrozy et al.\ propose a tripartite access control model for electronic health records that integrates GPT-based natural language processing, an ontology layer, and context-aware attribute-based access control (CAABAC)~\cite{nowrozy2025caabac}. Its use of GPT for policy inference and ontology-driven context extraction makes it a learned, data-driven approach to access decisions rather than a rule-based one. The model conditions access on a user's role attributes and the compliance and situational context present at the time of the request. A critical distinction from the approach proposed in the present thesis, however, is that CAABAC operates at the policy-decision layer: it determines whether access should be permitted but does not embed the risk assessment into the cryptographic key material itself. The present thesis enforces context-derived risk attributes within CP-ABE session keys, ensuring that the restriction remains effective even if the overlying policy layer is bypassed or misconfigured.

Mavhemwa et al.\ develop a Na\"{i}ve Bayes-based adaptive continuous authentication prototype targeting young IoMT users on Android devices, drawing on physiological biometrics from smartwatches in conjunction with behavioural, environmental, and contextual signals~\cite{mavhemwa2025naivebayes}. At each authentication event, the model assesses risk and trust by evaluating device characteristics, network conditions, location data, and user habit patterns alongside demographic factors such as age and medical condition, dynamically selecting the most appropriate authenticator. The system shows that risk-based adaptive authentication is practical in an IoMT deployment. The reported false rejection rate of 19\% provides a usability reference point against which the false challenge rate of the risk model developed in the present thesis may be contextualised. The principal difference lies in scope: Mavhemwa et al.\ confine their risk assessment to the authentication decision itself, whereas the present thesis propagates the risk score into the CP-ABE encryption layer, enforcing the restriction cryptographically within the stored data.

Saleem et al.\ present the SmartIoT Hybrid ML model, which is the most closely related work to the approach proposed in the present thesis~\cite{saleem2025ml}. It couples a Random Forest classifier with attribute-based authentication for IoT access control, evaluating each access request on the basis of user attributes, login patterns, and behavioural signals, and achieves 86\% authentication accuracy and 96\% recall at a response latency of 112\,ms on low-power devices. The result confirms that machine learning and attribute-based security can be combined at a latency suitable for resource-constrained IoT devices. However, the scheme operates exclusively at the authentication decision layer: it determines whether to admit or reject a session but exercises no control over what an admitted session may subsequently decrypt. Sensitive data remains accessible to any session that satisfies the classifier, and the access decision carries no cryptographic enforcement at the data storage level.

All ML-based systems reviewed in this subsection operate at the detection, policy-decision, or authentication layer. Ullah et al.\ generate a threat alert; Anjum et al.\ update an access policy in response to detected anomalies; Nowrozy et al.\ adjust an access decision based on contextual compliance; Mavhemwa et al.\ select a risk-appropriate authenticator; and Saleem et al.\ admit or deny sessions based on attribute-enriched ML scores. None of these systems penetrates the encryption layer. Data remains accessible to any session that clears the detection or decision checkpoint, and policy revisions do not retroactively constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. The case for cryptographic enforcement rests on this observation: a CP-ABE key that is incapable of decrypting sensitive data provides a guarantee that holds independently of whether the detection system has been deceived.

\section{Critical Analysis and Research Gaps}
\label{sec:section_CriticalAnalysis}

\textbf{Static cryptographic access control.} The literature reviewed above reveals three specific gaps, each corresponding directly to the problem statement and research questions of the present thesis. The first concerns static cryptographic access control: a recent high-performing CP-ABE scheme for IoMT, the scheme of Ding et al.~\cite{ding2026badsabe}, grants decryption rights to any session holding the requisite attributes, irrespective of the context in which access is requested. The encryption layer has no awareness of login behaviour or access risk. A credential stolen from a healthcare worker therefore confers the same cryptographic access as the worker's own key, with no mechanism at the data layer to prevent unauthorised decryption.

\textbf{Authentication that stops at the login boundary.} Both ASMR~\cite{xu2025asmr} and PQ-CLSCL~\cite{xu2025pqclscl} give strong guarantees about identity and data integrity, but their protective scope ends at the moment of authentication. Once a session is admitted, neither scheme has any further influence over what it may decrypt. A stolen credential with a valid signature passes every check just as the legitimate owner's credential would.

\textbf{ML-driven security decisions that do not reach the encryption layer.} Ullah et al.~\cite{ullah2025interpretable} and Anjum et al.~\cite{anjum2025blockchain} both demonstrate that machine learning can produce accurate, adaptive security decisions within IoMT environments. However, in each case the respective system operates at the detection or policy-decision layer, leaving the encryption layer unaffected. Data remains accessible to any session that is admitted or that circumvents the detection point, and policy revisions do not constrain what an already-authenticated session may decrypt. Context-aware cryptographic access control governed by a learned risk model remains unaddressed in the literature surveyed here.


The methodology developed in the present thesis to address this gap, covering the risk model, the CP-ABE integration design, and the experimental evaluation protocol, is detailed in Chapter~3.

\section{Summary of the Chapter}
\label{sec:section_LitSum}

This chapter has reviewed the most pertinent recent literature on cryptographic access control for IoMT, using the scheme of Ding et al.\ as the primary reference for assessing what CP-ABE can and cannot achieve in practice. Authentication schemes for IoMT were examined and found, without exception, to treat authentication as a one-time binary decision with no subsequent influence over the decryption layer. Machine learning approaches to IoMT security were surveyed and shown to produce accurate and adaptive decisions, yet none carries those decisions into the encryption layer where data is protected. Three specific research gaps were identified across these threads, each directly connected to the problem statement, research questions, and objectives of the present thesis. The methodology developed to address these gaps is presented in the following chapter.
